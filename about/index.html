About

Research

I am an MD PhD student at Stanford University, advised by Roxana Daneshjou, and funded by the Knight-Hennessy Scholarship.

I think three of the most important problems of our time are:

  1. understanding how language models work
  2. evaluating them rigorously
  3. designing policies that allow their rapid but safe adoption

To address these, I focus on capabilities research, eval frameworks, and safe deployment strategies.

Before Stanford, I did my BA at Johns Hopkins, where I worked on genetically engineering stem cells into specialized cell types, high-throughput omics for circadian rhythms (with Ted Dawson and Sung Ung Kang), and AI for drug design.

Incarceration policy

I worked on incarceration reform for more than a decade, as a teacher, researcher, and advocate.

Education: With the Jail Tutorial Project, I founded GED, ESOL, and college credit-granting programs at numerous prisons/jails. I also created job training programs for post-release.

Women’s health: With advocacy organizations, I worked on healthcare access in prisons and jails. Our work helped lead to Maryland finally enacting law such that incarcerated women must receive free tampons/pads (HB 797 / SB 598, 2018).

Bail reform: With Doug Colbert at the University of Maryland’s law school, I helped create programs that offered people accused of non-violent crimes alternatives to pretrial incarceration, sparing them and their families the financial and personal toll that detention brings.

School-to-prison pipeline: I worked with public schools/local businesses to create after-school activities, and researched education policy and transportation modeling at JHU School of Education.

Academic service

Career: Grateful to have mentored dozens of students on career planning and on pitching themselves for grants and medical school admissions, including editing essays and writing rec letters.
